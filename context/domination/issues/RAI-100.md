# RAI-100 — Rail: GTFS times to instants

**Scope:** Turn a GTFS service date and time into an instant, measured from noon minus 12 hours in the agency's zone, so clock-change days come out right.

## Gap

A GTFS time is not measured from midnight. "The time is measured from 'noon minus 12h' of the
service day (effectively midnight except for days on which daylight savings time changes occur)"
([painpoints § Rail — GTFS is the gap](../painpoints.md#gtfs-is-the-gap)). On a spring-forward day
noon minus 12 hours is 23:00 on the day before; on a fall-back day it is 01:00. The definition
exists so that feeds stay correct on those days without editing.

A consumer that anchors at local midnight is wrong by an hour for every trip on those two days a
year, and on a spring-forward day in a zone that changes at midnight it has no midnight to anchor
to. The times are also in one zone for the whole feed: "The times provided in stop_times.txt are in
the timezone specified by agency.agency_timezone, not stop_timezone."

([GTFS Schedule Reference, Field Types: Time and Date; `stops.txt` `stop_timezone`](https://gtfs.org/documentation/schedule/reference/))

## Scope

- `packages/gmt/src/rail/convert/gtfsToInstant.ts`:
  - `gtfsToInstant(serviceDate: string, gtfsTime: string, timeZone: string): string` — The UTC
    instant, ending in `Z`, that `gtfsTime` names on `serviceDate` in `timeZone`.
    - `serviceDate` is the GTFS `Date` form `YYYYMMDD` or an ISO `YYYY-MM-DD`.
    - `gtfsTime` is a GTFS `Time` as `parseGtfsTime` reads it, including values past `24:00:00`.
    - `timeZone` is the feed's `agency_timezone`, an IANA name. It is required: GMT does not resolve
      an agency or a stop to a zone.
- The `@northguild/gmt/rail/convert` subpath (`package.json` `exports` and `typesVersions`, the dox
  `gmt-modules.ts` barrel).

### The rule

1. Check `serviceDate`'s shape: exactly `YYYYMMDD` or `YYYY-MM-DD`. Only a value in that shape
   goes on, to `Temporal.PlainDate.from`, which checks the calendar.
2. Resolve 12:00 on `serviceDate` in `timeZone` with `resolveLocal` and `disambiguation: "reject"`.
3. Subtract 12 hours of exact time.
4. Add `dayOverflow × 86400 + seconds` of exact time.

Steps 3 and 4 are exact durations, not wall-clock moves: GTFS measures the time from that instant.

### What returns the sentinel

- A `serviceDate` that is neither form, or names a day that does not exist.
- A `gtfsTime` `parseGtfsTime` rejects.
- A `timeZone` that is not a valid IANA name or offset.
- A noon that the zone skipped or repeated on that date, which `"reject"` refuses.

## Tool

**GTFS Service Day Clock.** Enter a service date, a GTFS time and the agency's zone and see the
instant and the local reading; on a clock-change date the tool draws the noon-minus-12-hours anchor
beside a naive midnight anchor and marks the hour where they disagree. Calls `gtfsToInstant` and
`parseGtfsTime`. Teaches: a GTFS day starts at noon minus 12 hours, which is midnight only on most
days.

## Design notes

- **The anchor is noon minus 12 hours, not midnight.** Clock changes are made at night, so on the
  days they happen noon still occurs once; that is why GTFS measures from it. Using local midnight
  brings back the bug the definition was written to avoid.
- **Disambiguation is `"reject"`, and it is stated.** The only local time this function resolves is
  noon. If a zone's history skips or repeats noon on a date, there is no anchor GTFS defines, so the
  function returns the sentinel rather than guess; the JSDoc states the policy in
  [LOCAL_TIME_RESOLUTION.md](../../reference/LOCAL_TIME_RESOLUTION.md)'s words.
- **The result can fall on another local date.** `00:00:00` on a spring-forward service day is 23:00
  local on the day before, and `25:35:00` is 01:35 on the day after. Both are what GTFS defines.
- **No new format, so no public validator or pattern.** The time is RAI-99's and is checked by
  `isValidGtfsTime`. The service date's shape is checked first, as exactly `YYYYMMDD` or
  `YYYY-MM-DD`, by an anchored regex in `internal/gtfsServiceDate.ts`; only a value in that shape
  goes to `Temporal.PlainDate.from`, which then checks the calendar. Temporal alone would accept
  too much: it also reads date-times, extended years, calendar annotations and zoned date-times,
  all as a date. The zone is Temporal's to check.
- **One zone per feed.** GTFS puts every `stop_times` value in `agency_timezone`, so the caller
  passes that zone even for a stop with its own `stop_timezone`.
- Finding which service dates to query for a wall-clock window is not part of this story.

## Authority — what each rule rests on

GTFS is an open specification, maintained by MobilityData with its community and published by
agencies in many countries; the reference was read at gtfs.org.

| Rule | Source |
| --- | --- |
| A time is measured from noon minus 12 hours of the service day | GTFS Reference, Field Types, Time: "The time is measured from 'noon minus 12h' of the service day (effectively midnight except for days on which daylight savings time changes occur)" |
| The service date is `YYYYMMDD` | GTFS Reference, Field Types, Date: "Service day in the YYYYMMDD format" |
| Times are in the agency's zone | GTFS Reference, `stops.txt` `stop_timezone`: "The times provided in stop_times.txt are in the timezone specified by agency.agency_timezone, not stop_timezone" |
| Only `YYYYMMDD` and `YYYY-MM-DD` are read; the shape is checked before Temporal | **GMT rule.** `Temporal.PlainDate.from` also reads date-times, extended years and annotations |
| The ISO form `YYYY-MM-DD` is also read | **GMT rule.** ISO 8601 is GMT's input; the GTFS form is ISO 8601's basic format |
| A skipped or repeated noon returns the sentinel | **GMT rule**, on [LOCAL_TIME_RESOLUTION.md](../../reference/LOCAL_TIME_RESOLUTION.md): a local time is never resolved silently |
| Exact-time subtraction and addition | TC39 Temporal `Instant.prototype.add` and `subtract`, which move exact time |
| Sentinels | [coding-standards § API Contract](../../coding-standards.md#api-contract) |

## What gmt provides (do not re-implement)

- `resolveLocal` from CORE-4 — resolving noon on the service date with `"reject"`
- `parseGtfsTime` from RAI-99 — reading the GTFS time, including its overflow

## Verification

- `gtfsToInstant('20240130', '25:35:00', 'America/Los_Angeles')` returns `2024-01-31T09:35:00Z`, 01:35 local on 31 January
- `gtfsToInstant('20240130', '27:00:00', 'America/Los_Angeles')` returns `2024-01-31T11:00:00Z`, 03:00 local on the next day
- Spring forward: `gtfsToInstant('20240331', '03:00:00', 'Europe/Berlin')` returns `2024-03-31T01:00:00Z` (03:00 local), and `'00:00:00'` returns `2024-03-30T22:00:00Z` — 23:00 local on 30 March, not midnight; `'14:30:00'` returns `2024-03-31T12:30:00Z`, 14:30 local
- Fall back: `gtfsToInstant('20240407', '00:00:00', 'Australia/Sydney')` returns `2024-04-06T14:00:00Z` (01:00 local, daylight time), and `'12:00:00'` returns `2024-04-07T02:00:00Z`, noon local
- `gtfsToInstant('2024-01-30', '25:35:00', 'America/Los_Angeles')` returns the same as the `YYYYMMDD` form
- A naive midnight anchor gives a different answer from every spring-forward and fall-back row above, asserted
- A service date of `'20240230'`, `'2024-01-30T23:00'`, `'20240130T2300'`, `'+002024-01-30'` or `'2024-01-30[u-ca=hebrew]'`, a time of `'25:60:00'`, a time zone of `'Not/AZone'`, and an empty zone return the sentinel
- `pnpm run validate` stays green
