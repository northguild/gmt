# AV-97 — Aviation: SSIM dates

**Scope:** Read and write the `DDMMMYY` date of airline schedule files, with English month letters and a century the caller states.

## Gap

Airline schedules are exchanged in IATA's Standard Schedules Information Manual (SSIM) formats,
and their dates are not ISO 8601. A schedule file's period of operation is written `DDMMMYY`:
two digits of day, the first three letters of the month in English, upper case, and two digits of
year — `01JAN26` (IATA's Chapter 7 layout is not reached; see Authority). Slot messages use
the same day and month letters without the year: "The last three are the first three letters of
the name of the month in English: JAN, FEB, …, NOV, DEC" ([painpoints § Aviation, SSIM
dates](../painpoints.md#aviation)). Schedules are filed against seasons and carry dates a year
ahead and decades back ([painpoints § Aviation, schedule seasons](../painpoints.md#aviation)).

Two defects follow. A parser that reads the month through the platform's locale breaks on any
machine whose default locale is not English. A parser that picks a century itself is wrong for
either the archive or the forward schedule.

(IATA, _Standard Schedules Information Manual_, Chapter 7 — not reached. AECFA (Spain), _Quick Guide
to Using the IATA SSIM format_, Chapter 6 slot messages, a national coordinator's guide, is cited
by name for the day and month letters; it is no longer online (the link returns 404 and no
archived copy exists). The seven-character form rests on the sources in
[Authority](#authority--what-each-rule-rests-on).)

## Scope

- `packages/gmt/src/aviation/parse/parseSsimDate.ts`:
  - `parseSsimDate(value: string, options: TwoDigitYearOptions): string` — `DDMMMYY` to an ISO
    date. Without `options.yearWindow` the result is the sentinel.
- `packages/gmt/src/aviation/format/formatSsimDate.ts`:
  - `formatSsimDate(value: string, options: TwoDigitYearOptions): string` — An ISO date to
    `DDMMMYY`, upper case. Returns the sentinel for a year outside the window, so a round trip never
    changes a century.
- `packages/gmt/src/aviation/validate/isValidSsimDate.ts`:
  - `isValidSsimDate(value: string, options: TwoDigitYearOptions): boolean` — True exactly when
    `parseSsimDate` returns a date for the same arguments; it calls the parser, so the two cannot
    disagree. False without `yearWindow`, and for a value that is not a string.
- `internal/ssimDate.ts` — the `DDMMMYY` regex and the table of twelve month letters it is built
  from. Not in `regex/`: IATA publishes no pattern. It joins `context/coding-standards.md` as a
  scoped exception, as INT-15's EDI grammars did.
- `TwoDigitYearOptions` is the type INT-15 defined; this story adds no type.
- This story opens the `aviation/` realm: the `@northguild/gmt/aviation`,
  `@northguild/gmt/aviation/parse`, `@northguild/gmt/aviation/format` and
  `@northguild/gmt/aviation/validate` subpaths (`package.json` `exports` and `typesVersions`, the
  dox `gmt-modules.ts` barrel), and the Aviation industry in `apps/dox/src/data/industries.json`,
  with namespace `aviation`; its glyph in `INDUSTRY_ICON_PATHS`
  (`apps/dox/src/lib/industry-tags.ts`), and its hub text in
  `apps/dox/src/lib/industry-overview.ts`: three to five problems, each answered by a function the

  new industry takes, and `industry-tags.test.ts` and `industry-overview.test.ts` fail without them.

### What returns the sentinel

- `parseSsimDate`: a value that is not two digits, three upper-case English month letters and two
  digits; lower or mixed case; a month name in another language; a day that does not exist in the
  month and windowed year; no `yearWindow`.
- `formatSsimDate`: a value that is not an ISO 8601 calendar date; a year outside the window; no
  `yearWindow`.

## Tool

**SSIM Date Reader.** Enter a `DDMMMYY` date and a window and see the ISO date; switch the page's
locale and see the answer stay the same. A second panel writes a date back. Calls `parseSsimDate`,
`formatSsimDate`. Teaches: a format's month letters are part of the format, not a translation.

## Design notes

- **The month letters are fixed English and never go through `Intl`.** They are a code, not a
  translation. The twelve are a constant table in `internal/ssimDate.ts`, and the JSDoc says the
  function is locale-independent: an explicit exception to the library's locale-aware formatting,
  with a test over the full locale matrix proving the ambient locale changes nothing.
- **Upper case only.** Every example in the guides read is upper case, and a reader that accepts
  `jan` invites a writer that emits it.
- **The century is the caller's.** SSIM does not say which century `YY` belongs to, and schedule
  files hold dates both ahead and behind. The caller states `yearWindow`, as INT-15's pattern parsers
  require; the library holds no cut-off of its own.
- **A round trip never changes a century.** `formatSsimDate` refuses a year outside the window.
- **The validator takes the window too.** `29FEB00` is valid with a window from 2000 and not with
  one from 1900, so `isValidSsimDate` takes the parser's options and is false without
  `yearWindow`. The grammar stays in `internal/`: a value is valid only once its century is known,
  which no pattern can say.
- **The Chapter 7 text is not reached.** IATA does not publish SSIM openly. The JSDoc says so in
  one line. The sources that stand in for it are in Authority.
- Seasons, days of operation and leg times are not part of this story.

## Authority — what each rule rests on

IATA is a multinational industry body; SSIM is its standard. The manual is not publicly
available.

| Rule | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Day as two digits, month as three upper-case English letters | AECFA (Spain), _Quick Guide to Using the IATA SSIM format_, Chapter 6: "The first two are the day of the month. Shown as 01, 02,….30, 31"; "The last three are the first three letters of the name of the month in English: JAN, FEB, ……, NOV, DEC". The guide is no longer online, so it is cited by name only. ACL (UK), [_An Introductory Guide to SSIM_](https://www.acl-uk.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/SSIM-Review-Final2.pdf) (April 2021), a condensed extract of Chapter 6, shows the same form (`01APR`, `10JAN`). Corroboration; IATA SSIM Chapter 6 not reached |
| Two digits of year after the month (`DDMMMYY`) | Only EUROCONTROL, [_CSST Users Guide_](https://www.eurocontrol.int/archive_download/all/node/9248) Ed. 8.0 (28-04-2021), §3.2 Figure 1, shows the seven-character form (`05APR10`, `11APR10`, `16DEC09`). Avinor's [SSIM description](https://xwiki.avinor.no/x/rYC5Ag) shows only a two-digit year ("ddmmyy"). IATA SSIM Chapter 7 was not reached. The owner accepted these two sources as meeting the story's bar |
| No `Intl` for the month | **GMT rule.** The letters are a code fixed in English |
| The century is the caller's | **GMT rule**, INT-15's: no standard names the century of a two-digit year |
| The grammar is internal; the public check is `isValidSsimDate` | **GMT rule**, INT-15's: IATA publishes no pattern |
| `isValidSsimDate` is true exactly when the parser returns a date | **GMT rule.** The validator calls the parser, so the two cannot disagree |
| A year outside the window is refused by the formatter | **GMT rule.** Writing it would read back as another year |
| A day that does not exist is rejected | TC39 Temporal `overflow: "reject"`; [coding-standards § Calendar & zone semantics](../../coding-standards.md#calendar--zone-semantics) |
| Sentinels | [coding-standards § API Contract](../../coding-standards.md#api-contract) |

## What gmt provides (do not re-implement)

- `TwoDigitYearOptions` from INT-15 — the caller-stated hundred-year window
- `internal/twoDigitYear.ts` and `internal/yearWindowStart.ts` from INT-15 — placing a two-digit
  year in that window, and reading the option
- `isDateString` (`internal/inputChecks.ts`), the check `isValidDate` uses — the ISO date the formatter takes
- `isOptionsArgument` (`internal/isObject.ts`) — an options argument that is not an object gives the sentinel

## Verification

- `parseSsimDate('01JAN26', { yearWindow: 2000 })` returns `2026-01-01`; with no `yearWindow` it returns the sentinel
- `parseSsimDate('29FEB24', { yearWindow: 2000 })` returns `2024-02-29`; `'29FEB25'` and `'31APR26'` return the sentinel
- `parseSsimDate('01jan26', …)`, `'01Jan26'`, `'01JANV26'`, `'1JAN26'` and `'01ENE26'` return the sentinel
- `parseSsimDate` returns the same answer under every locale in the full locale matrix
- `formatSsimDate('2026-10-25', { yearWindow: 2000 })` returns `25OCT26`; `formatSsimDate('1999-12-31', { yearWindow: 2000 })` returns the sentinel
- Round-trip: `parseSsimDate(formatSsimDate(d, w), w)` returns `d` for a sample of dates inside the window `w`: every day of 2024 and 2025 under window 2000, the first and last day of every month under window 1950, and the edges of windows 0 and 9900. The reverse direction, `formatSsimDate(parseSsimDate(s, w), w) === s`, is swept over all 1,462 valid `DD<MMM>YY` strings for years 00, 24, 25 and 99. The window holds about 36,500 days, and checking each would break the per-test time budget
- `isValidSsimDate('01JAN26', { yearWindow: 2000 })` is `true`; `isValidSsimDate('01jan26', { yearWindow: 2000 })`, `isValidSsimDate('31APR26', { yearWindow: 2000 })` and `isValidSsimDate('01JAN26', {})` are `false`
- `isValidSsimDate` agrees with `parseSsimDate` on every parse row above, under every locale in the matrix; a number and `null` are `false`
- `pnpm run validate` stays green

## Decisions

- **The two-digit year is sourced well enough.** EUROCONTROL's CSST guide (§3.2 Figure 1) and
  Avinor (a two-digit year only) meet the bar of public sources from two countries, so `DDMMMYY` is
  built as asserted. Only the EUROCONTROL guide shows the seven-character form; Avinor shows a
  two-digit year alone. The JSDoc keeps one line saying IATA's Chapter 7 text was not reached.
- **The round trip is tested on a sample**, not on every date: the window holds about 36,500
  days, and three polyfill calls on each would break the per-test time budget. The sample is
  given under Verification.
- **`DDMMM` with no year and `00XXX00` are out of scope.** A test shows `00XXX00` returning `""`
  because `XXX` is not a month. No JSDoc or docs page says what `00XXX00` means in SSIM.
- **The icon is a control tower** (`industry-aviation`).
- **Upper case only.** A reader that accepts `jan` invites a writer that emits it, and folding
  case can map other characters onto a month code (`"ſEP".toUpperCase() === "SEP"`) and depends on
  the locale (`"i".toLocaleUpperCase("tr")`). No case folding, no trimming.
- **ASCII digits only.** The grammar writes `[0-9]`, not `\d`, as the X12 and EDIFACT siblings do.
- **No `Intl` for the month.** The letters are a constant table in `internal/ssimDate.ts`, a
  separate literal and not derived from `ENGLISH_MONTH_NAMES` (a different authority, title
  case, and deriving it would need a case conversion). The regex is built once from the table.
- **The century is the caller's.** `options` is required in the type; `yearWindow` has no default;
  an omitted or empty one, or an options argument that is not an object, gives the sentinel.
- **The grammar stays internal.** IATA publishes no pattern, and a value is valid only once its
  century is known, so nothing is added to `regex/`. `isValidSsimDate` calls the parser, so the
  two cannot disagree.
- **The formatter refuses a year outside the window**, and takes only an ISO calendar date; a
  date-time, an instant or another calendar gives `""`.
- **Days that do not exist are rejected** by Temporal with `overflow: "reject"`; the field reader
  checks no date itself.
- **Loop cost:** none of the three functions loops, so there are no bench rows.
- **Aviation `drawsOn` is empty and there is no `calculate` module.** A `gmt-aviation` skill
  was created. AV-98 adds its METAR and NOTAM functions to this realm.
- **Out of scope:** days of operation, seasons, leg times and Record Types 1 to 5 as records.
