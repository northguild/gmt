# Tier 4: globe engine and landing hero

Part of [built/index.md](index.md). Zone clocks, filters, scrubber, boundary data and credits: [tier-4-zones.md](tier-4-zones.md). Verify any globe change with `pnpm globe:smoke` (needs a GPU; CI has none and a software adapter, `GLOBE_SMOKE_SOFTWARE=1`, cannot gate).

## Engine (`src/lib/globe/`)

- `globe/` knows nothing about time: it draws a planet, takes markers, regions and arcs, and asks the caller for the instant. `src/lib/globe.ts` is the Dox layer (zones, clocks, selection, tooltip) and keeps `initGlobe(host, clockPanel)` for the hero, `/tools/zoned-earth/` and the `/dox` rail.
- One pinhole camera for every globe: gnomonic, `P` = 6.61 Earth radii (geostationary altitude, `pinhole.ts`). No orthographic path and no camera-distance option exist. The shaders, the fallback and the sky take their constants from `pinhole.ts`. `sky-one-camera` proves the sky and globe share centre and radius; `sky-from-black` and `sky-follows-drag` the rotation. Drag gain is 90 degrees per radius.
- `controller.ts` takes timestamps as arguments so both renderers feel the same and a fake clock drives it in tests.
- Backend choice: the engine asks for a device before touching a canvas, because `getContext("webgpu")` claims an element for good. A null adapter, rejected device, shader compile failure or first-frame validation error means canvas-2D. A lost device is retried once while visible, then the fallback holds. `?globe=webgpu|canvas2d` pins one.
- Canvas 2D draws in a worker (`canvas2d.worker.ts`, `OffscreenCanvas`, protocol in `protocol.ts`). If `canDrawOffThread()` fails, the worker errors, answers `failed` or is not ready in 8 s, the main-thread renderer loads; `GlobeOptions.offThread: false` forces it. `text-parity.ts` compares a DOM canvas label with an `OffscreenCanvas` label and throws on any byte difference. The painter never calls `clip()` (anti-aliasing differs); the globe canvas sets `-webkit-font-smoothing: auto` (`globe-canvas-smoothing.test.ts`).
- Trap: error scopes never span an `await`. Every globe shares the device, so an open scope catches another globe's errors. `withValidation` takes synchronous work only; `createCheckedShaderModule` pops before it awaits (`webgpu/device.test.ts`).
- The WGSL is built from template strings with constants from `shading.ts`; `webgpu/shaders.test.ts` checks each arrives. Coverage uses blend op `max` so translucent layers never double-blend.
- Geometry: rings are unwrapped at the antimeridian, densified, triangulated with `earcut` plus red-green refinement. Edges are straight in lng/lat (RFC 7946 3.1.1), which is also how a tap resolves to a zone. A hole is aligned to its outer ring's 360-degree window first, or the mesh loses a band.
- `renderer-graph.test.ts` keeps `d3-geo` only in the canvas-2D fallback. A WebGPU browser never downloads `d3-geo`; one without it never downloads shaders or `earcut`.
- Spin: 2 degrees a second, eases in over 2 s every start. While only the spin moves the globe it draws at most every 30 ms. The wheel is swallowed only while zoom can still change. `rAF` and the 1 s tick stop on `visibilitychange`; reduced motion gives a static globe with selection working.
- Every instance mounts lazily through `IntersectionObserver`. The landing globe (`[data-space]`) then waits for `load` and `requestIdleCallback` (`whenIdle`, `yield-to-main.ts`), yields between init steps and parses boundaries in 8 ms slices (`createSlicer`) so no task passes 50 ms (INP and TBT).
- `hero-space.ts` loads `getUnixNow` and `getSystemTimeZone` by dynamic import inside the star-list `Promise.all`. A static import moves LCP by about a second.
- Shading (`shading.ts`): per-pixel from sun elevation. Both washes are tokens that `gmt-a11y.css` zeroes under reduced transparency and raised contrast; `globe.ts` re-reads the theme when those preferences or forced colours change.

## Controls and zone drawer

- `renderGlobeTemplate` (`src/lib/globe-mount.ts`, shared by `Globe.astro` and the rail) draws one `role="group"` named "Globe controls": `+`, `-`, reset, the zone finder's magnifier (space mode only) and the data-credits info button, in that tab order (`data-gate="globe-zoom"`). Every globe carries the info button; its popover id is `<idPrefix>-data-credits`.
- Space mode (`<Globe space />`): no frame; the engine paints into `.globeLayer`. `createGlobe` options are opt-in: `framing`, `hitArea` (the one tab stop; the canvas never is), `maxDpr`, `pauseWhenHidden`, `pauseWhile(hold)` (`globe-cover.ts` watches the search dialog and mobile menu with one `MutationObserver`, no polling) and `onInteract(kind)`. A wheel or touch off the hit area belongs to the page.
- Side by side is `(width >= 64rem) and (orientation: landscape)`; stacked is the rest. `frameSpace` (`frameBand` in `lib/globe-frame.ts`) shrinks the disc only as far as the stack needs (`STACK_CLEARANCE`, 8 px); stacked, the disc sits above the zoom row, its atmosphere whole, and `framingWatch` measures again on any size change. The `hero-space` `position` step proves placement at 390, 768, 1280, 1440, 1920, 2560 and 3008.
- Trap: the standalone sizing block in `globe.module.css` (`.root:not([data-space])`) must never reach the hero; reaching it collapsed the stage between 56rem and 80rem.
- Trap: the hero's pull-up under the header sits in `@layer gmt.shell`, because Starlight's markdown spacing gives later blocks a top margin in a later layer.
- No layout shift: Michroma loads from inlined bytes before first paint (`src/lib/display-face.ts`); the copy's CSS lives in `HeroCopy.astro`'s own unlayered `<style>`; `hero-space` asserts a layout-shift total of exactly 0 over 5 s on a cold cache at 1440, 1920 and 390.
- Nothing invisible covers the Earth: copy wrappers have `pointer-events: none`, and only the eyebrow, title, lede and three buttons take events. `hero-space` samples points beside the text.
- Drawer: off on load (`inert`). Locked, the first globe interaction opens it, focus unchanged; stacked, where the sheet would cover the page, only the magnifier does. The magnifier focuses search. Close, Escape and a press outside the Earth's disc (`getCentre()`, not the canvas box) shut it; only the magnifier or a tap on a zone reopens it. A click on a zone runs `chooseZone` (`lib/zone-action.ts`), the one path shared by the list, the search and the Earth. While open, the zoom stack is `inert` and `visibility: hidden` at every width; focus returns to the magnifier on close. The Settings gear and Data credits sit inside inert regions while the drawer is shut, so `focus()` is a no-op there; keyboard order is magnifier, then Settings. Escape order: suggestions, Settings panel, drawer.
- The zone card is a manual popover shown at mount and hidden with `hidden`, so it draws in the top layer above the header. Inside inert content (`[inert] .tooltip`) it hides, since Starlight 0.41's mobile menu inerts `.main-frame`. It keeps clear of the close (`tooltipLimit`) or hides (`data-occluded`) behind the drawer.
- Look at (landing only): a kit `disclosure`, icon only, in the zoom stack's `role="group"` after Reset zoom (`lib/sky-view.ts`). Its panel is a second bar drawn like the stack (`widgets/tools/sky-look.module.css`): one button per row of `SKY_BODIES`, in the registry's order (the Sun, the Moon, then the planets outward from the Sun), each with its icon or astronomical symbol and its name, `aria-pressed` on the held body. Reason: the stack is where the camera controls already are, and a labelled bar reads at a glance where an icon-only second stack would not. The toggle is one of the stack's own buttons (`globe.module.css` styles every `button` under `.zoom`; `sky-look.module.css` undoes only what the disclosure adds, and its row rules weigh (0,3,0) so they beat the stack's square buttons and its hover fill). The stack and the bar lay the see-through border tint over the button surface, so a body behind them does not shine through the 1 px dividers. `.zoom` stops clipping while the bar is open so it can sit beside the stack, towards the globe, its foot level with the stack's (above the row on a coarse pointer). A planet is `aria-disabled`, its reason in `title`, while the planet layer loads or while it is too faint to be drawn; the rows never come and go, so the bar never jumps. The globe's tooltip is a top-layer popover that no z-index beats, so it hides while the bar is open. Choosing sets the lens zoom, then flies the one camera with the hop (the hop returns to the controller's goal zoom, so the zoom goes first), closes the bar and returns focus to the toggle, which stays cyan (`data-held`) while a body is held. The stack shows only with the sheet closed on a stacked phone, so the bar opens over the sky bay and the sheet is never touched. Hidden in forced colours with the sky.
- The aim (`lib/sky-aim.ts`, pure): the largest of zoom 2 to 1 whose spot beside the limb is 16 px inside the Earth's area and clear of the copy, open drawer and zoom stack, found by Newton's method on the rotation; the Moon is aimed through `moonFromCamera`, since its parallax is about a degree. No spot: look past the Earth at the body at zoom 1. `heroAimFrame` (`lib/hero-space.ts`) measures in the hero's `[data-space]`, not the hero: stacked, the hero runs the page's full height and the Earth's row ends at the fold, so a body is never put under the pages. Stacked, the copy column is `display: contents` and has no box, so the aim and the meteor keep clear of each of its cells instead. The open bar is one more box to keep clear of (`globe-mount.ts` passes it to `heroAimFrame`).
- The hold: on globe frames, at most once a second of the sky's clock, never mid-flight; a body 8 px off its spot, or a box that meets something, re-aims without a hop. `engine.holdAmbient(true)` keeps the idle spin off while the view is on (15 lines in the controller); release schedules it `IDLE_MS` later. The spin only runs at rest zoom, so after a release above zoom 1 it returns once the reader zooms out.
- Release never moves the camera: choosing the held body again, a drag, pinch, wheel, tap or key, a zoom-stack button or a picked zone. Reason: the reader's own move wins over the view.
- Names (landing only, `lib/sky-names.ts`): a pointer over bare sky (the hero, `[data-space]` or `globe-hit`) within 12 px of a body's disc for a mouse, 28 px for a touch, shows its name in a glass chip above it, a planet's with a dim " · Planet"; below it within 48 px of the window's top, held 4 px inside the window's sides. Reason: northguild.dev names its sky the same way. The chip is a manual popover (the zone card's pattern), so no hero transform or clip moves it, placed in window pixels and hidden on scroll. Only drawn bodies are named: a planet the layer leaves out, a body wholly inside the Earth's disc, a planet inside the Moon's. The nearest disc edge wins, and a tie goes to the later row, so the Moon wins over the Sun it covers. It follows on `engine.onFrame`; a drag, any globe gesture but a tap, the copy or a control, leaving the window (not a lifted finger: a tap's name stays until the next tap) and forced colours take it away. `aria-hidden`, since Look at names the same bodies to every reader.
- `data-credits-check` expects the info button sixth on `/` (Look at sits before the magnifier).
- Hero copy: `HeroCopy` takes an optional `eyebrow` (`HeroGlobe` passes `@northguild/gmt`; Starlight's hero schema has none, so keep it out of front matter). The `Prompt` button's accessible name is `Copy prompt` until `Copied!` or `Copy failed` shows.
- The hero is a dark island (`.gmt-herostage` carries `.gmt-theme-dark`). Keeping it true takes four things: every light token has a dark counterpart; each `gmt-a11y.css` block that restates a token for `:root` names the island too; a light-only rule that can reach inside excludes it with `:not(:where(.gmt-theme-dark *))`; a dark-only rule the hero needs names the island beside `[data-theme="dark"]`. No unit test reads these; `pnpm globe:smoke` compares computed colours across themes.

- Stacked (`globe-phone.module.css`), the drawer is a bottom sheet; `followViewport` (`lib/globe-sheet.ts`) keeps it above the iOS keyboard, opening it never scrolls the page, and coarse-pointer targets are 44px. The zone card keeps between the stage's top and the open sheet (`cardBand`).

## Night sky (landing only)

- `HeroSpace.astro` holds three `aria-hidden`, `pointer-events: none` canvases (`gmt-space.css`): Milky Way, stars, meteor. `lib/sky.ts` (`mountSky`) has no loop of its own: the first `view` makes the first draw and sets `data-sky="ready"`; after that it redraws only when the view's centre moves 0.01 degree, the Earth's centre or radius half a pixel, or zoom 0.002. `skyWithoutGlobe` draws once if the globe fails. Size caps, minified and gzipped: sky 5,216 bytes (the split into `sky-stars`, `sky-band` and `sky-bodies` for the 400-line rule costs about 120), Moon painter 3,200, Sun painter 2,048, meteor 4,096 (`scripts/sky-size.test.ts`).
- Star dots scale with the Earth's on-screen radius (`skyLens`), so a phone's are smaller; `hero-phone` reads it from `data-sky-camera`.
- `lib/sky-math.ts` holds the gnomonic projection, sidereal time (Meeus 12.4) and `starViewMatrix` (IAU 1976 precession). Stars and the Milky Way map are J2000; the Sun, Moon and sidereal time are of date. Tests pin Meeus examples and JPL Horizons rows (`sky-math.test.ts`).
- Stars: HYG v4.4 (pinned to commit `53e3df3`, hash checked), magnitude 5.5 and brighter, 2,865 stars in `src/assets/hero-sky.bin` (budget 24 KB), generated by `scripts/prepare-hero-sky.mjs`; the catalogue is never committed. It is CC BY-SA 4.0: `hero-sky.json` is the share-alike sidecar saying the repo's MIT licence does not cover it, and the credits popup lists "Stars" on `/` only.
- Milky Way: ESO panorama eso0932a (CC BY 4.0, credit "ESO/S. Brunier"), reduced by `scripts/prepare-hero-milkyway.mjs` (run by hand, refuses any other hash) to `src/assets/hero-milkyway.webp` (budget 40 KB). `hero-milkyway.json` is the credit sidecar (CC BY 4.0 asks for the credit as given and a note that the work was changed). A failed fetch or decode leaves the band out; stars still draw. Listed as "Milky Way" after "Stars".
- Sun and Moon draw on the stars canvas. The Moon is seen from the camera (`moonFromCamera`: parallax, size, phase), never from the Earth's centre, and is pinned to nine JPL Horizons observer rows. Pins sit at least 10 degrees from full (the limb is ill-conditioned near full). A body is drawn only inside the camera's field of view, and both sky canvases are clipped outside the Earth's disc.
- Moon map: `src/assets/sky-moon.webp` (1024x512, budget 80 KB) is NASA SVS's CGI Moon Kit `lroc_color_2k.jpg` (LRO LROC data, a national peer, labelled so in `MOON_IMAGERY`; no international body publishes a global mosaic), reduced by `scripts/prepare-sky-bodies.mjs`, which pins URL, bytes and sha256 and refuses any other file. Both prepare scripts read their pinned source through `scripts/prepare-source.mjs`, so the download, the `--<flag> <file>` override and the hash check live once.
- `loadSkyMap(src, signal)` in `lib/space-sky.ts` is the one fetch-and-decode (`createImageBitmap` with `colorSpaceConversion: "none"`); the Milky Way and both bodies use it. `skyInputsOf(holder, host, signal)` reads the page's `data-sky-src`, `data-milky-way`, `data-sun-map` and `data-moon-map` once, so the hero and the deck stage cannot drift. `sampleMap` (`lib/sky-map.ts`) is the one bilinear lookup, wrapping in longitude; the band, the Sun and the Moon use it.
- The body maps load late so the first paint never waits: after `sky.ready` and `whenIdle()`, `loadBodyMaps` walks the `SKY_BODIES` registry in `space-sky.ts` (one row per body: its map input and a `make` that imports the painter chunk), fetches each declared map and calls `sky.maps({ sun, moon })` once with the painters that loaded. A body whose fetch, decode or import fails stays flat while the other still textures; `data-sky-maps` is `ready` when any loaded, else `failed`, with no console error. It is skipped under `forced-colors: active` or an aborted signal. No preload link. `body-paint.ts` (Moon) and `sun-paint.ts` are separate lazy chunks, each with its own cap row in `sky-size.test.ts`, so `sky.ts` carries neither. A new body is a row in `SKY_BODIES`, a painter and a cap row.
- `moonFrame` (`lib/body-frame.ts`) is the IAU WGCCRE pole and prime meridian (Archinal et al. 2018), turned to the frame of date by `precessToDate`; it is pinned to JPL Horizons from the geocentre and from the geostationary camera, where the sub-observer point differs by more than 3 degrees. `createMoonPainter` (`body-paint.ts`, which also holds the helpers both painters share: `discSize`, `coverage`, `screenAxes` and the one-canvas `cachedDisc`) shades with Lommel-Seeliger plus earthshine (the flat Moon's alpha `shine`, decoded to linear light, added on the lit side too so no seam shows at the terminator) over the map's white (its 99.9th percentile, so a full Moon does not clip and the half Moon is darker; the map's tones are remapped in the WebP to the Lunar Sourcebook albedo anchors, cos(lat)-weighted percentiles over |lat| <= 70 degrees), turns the linear level to its sRGB encoding for the alpha (a canvas composites encoded values), tints to `ICE_TINT`, and caches the painted disc by radius, sun direction, sub-observer point and pole angle, so a still sky repaints nothing. Below `MIN_BODY`, or with no map, the flat path draws unchanged (a digest test holds it byte-identical).
- Sun map: `src/assets/sky-sun.webp` (256x256, budget 16 KB) is ESA's press image of Solar Orbiter's PHI (observed 22 March 2023, CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO, labelled in `SUN_IMAGERY`), cropped to 1.02 solar radii and reduced to one grey band by the same script, with the mosaic's tile vignetting divided out (`flattenDisc`: about 2 %, a grid on screen; the limb darkening kept), its white scaled to 255 and stored near-lossless (lossy WebP's blocks showed as a grid) (the SOAR FITS data is CC BY-NC, so none ships). The JPEG is an orange table over the continuum; `sun-colour-map.mjs` stops the script unless every channel of the table rises with brightness, so the grey keeps the continuum's order, and a test holds the shipped disc's rings falling to the limb. Solar north is up in the source. `sunPole` (`body-frame.ts`, IAU 2015 pole of date, pinned to Horizons) gives the disc's turn on screen; `createSunPainter` (`sun-paint.ts`) paints `ICE_TINT * sample / 255`, so nothing clips and the limb darkens, opaque with a coverage edge; it reads two map pixels inside the limb so the black past it draws no dark ring, and the glare stays the flat one. A Sun that is not painted draws the flat core unchanged.
- `SKY_CREDITS` (`lib/data-credits.ts`) is the one ordered list of sky credit rows; the globe popup, the deck's credits and `SkyCredits.astro` read it. `sky.ts` stays under 400 lines by handing the bodies to `sky-bodies.ts`, the band to `sky-band.ts` and the stars to `sky-stars.ts`; their code counts toward the sky's cap because they are imported statically.
- Entrance: `lib/arrival.ts` is the inline head script (`arrivalInlineSource`): it writes `data-entrance="wait"` before first paint (`static` after `ARRIVAL_FAILSAFE_MS`, 2.5 s) and `data-arrival` on `pagereveal`. `startHeroEntrance` / `runHeroEntrance` set `t0` when the sky has drawn and `dawnReady` settled (or 800 ms after navigation start), write `run`, call `startDawn`, and write `done` after `ENTRANCE_MS` (3 s). With `entrance: "dawn"` the engine draws no frame until `startDawn`. The zoom stack stays hidden until `data-ready`, except while it holds focus or an open credits panel (`.zoom:is(:focus-within, :has(:popover-open))`).
- Entrance CSS is `gmt-entrance-freeze.css` (`gmt.controls`). The hero copy is never held: it paints at first paint and its freeze sweeps over text already there, so the largest paint is not delayed. Gates: `hero-space` entrance; `globe-smoke` waits for `data-dawn="done"`.
- The meteor (`lib/space-fx.ts`): one about every minute, never over the copy, an open drawer or the Earth, and none while the tab is hidden, the hero is off screen or reduced motion is set.
- Media: `forced-colors: active` hides the whole layer. `prefers-contrast: more` and `prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce` hide the Milky Way and the Sun's glare. Reduced motion removes the meteor and ambient spin; stars still follow the reader's drag.
- `hero-space` checks credits, `sky-sun-face` (the Sun's centre is 8/255 brighter than 0.85 radii out), no draw before ready, one draw at the start view, the page's clock to 5 s, no redraw in 4 s at rest, drag and star motion, reduced motion, Sun and Moon placement with Playwright's clock pinned in Nairobi (from Helsinki or Berlin the start camera never sees them), and `sky-one-camera`.

## Earth imagery

- In the dark theme, and on the landing hero in both, the WebGPU globe wraps NASA Blue Marble (`src/assets/earth-blue-marble.webp`, loaded by `?url` at a hashed `/gmt/_astro/` URL, 4096x2048, about 530 KB, made by `scripts/prepare-globe-imagery.py`, which records source and licence). Public domain (17 U.S.C. 105); NASA asks for acknowledgement and no logo or implied endorsement. The credit is the `EARTH_IMAGERY` row of the credits panel.
- `webgpu/imagery.ts` loads it after the renderer starts, never awaited: the globe draws flat and is interactive, then cross-fades over 600 ms (at once under reduced motion). One reference-counted texture per device. A failed fetch, decode or upload leaves the flat globe and logs at `info`.
- Stored `rgba8unorm-srgb`; each mip is a render pass (WebGPU has no `generateMipmap`); the shader uses `textureSampleGrad` with analytic gradients, because screen-space derivatives of a wrapping longitude pick the smallest mip and draw a seam.
- The "Natural colours" switch (fieldset "Look") sets `data-look="natural"` on `<html>` (`dox:look` in `localStorage`, `src/lib/look.ts`, every access guarded; `ThemeProvider.astro` applies it before first paint). It turns the Earth to its photograph and the Moon and Sun to their own colours (Sun: near-white, r=g=b); the default ice look keeps the tint. `gmt-tokens.css` names the hero island too, since the island re-declares both tokens. Every globe, the hero sky and the deck stage watch `data-look` beside `data-theme` through one `watchTheme` (`globe/globe-theme.ts`). The painters key their cached disc on the look, and `paintLook()` gives ice whatever is saved under `prefers-contrast: more`. The switch is hidden (not disabled) where it does nothing (`lookSwitchOffered`): no textured Earth and no textured sky, the light theme's flat globes, raised contrast and forced colours.
- Imagery takes the sun as brightness (`imageryLight`, a 0.18 ambient floor) and replaces the day wash. The "cyber look" tokens `--gmt-globe-imagery-duotone` and `--gmt-globe-vector-overlay` ship at 1; the overlay is lit by `imageryLight` too, or night-side land blurs the terminator. Both at 0 give the plain photograph.
- Zoom fade: `IMAGERY_FADE_START_ZOOM` (2) to `IMAGERY_FADE_END_ZOOM` (2.7). Every imagery term scales by one weight, so at weight 0 the output equals the flat globe pixel for pixel. The graticule scales by `1 - weight`.
- `--gmt-globe-imagery-alpha` is 1 in dark and 0 in light; `gmt-a11y.css` zeroes it under `prefers-contrast: more` and `forced-colors`, and at 0 the image is never downloaded. A light-theme reader who never opens the front page never downloads it.
- Legibility on every globe: each marker, ring, label, outline and arc sits on a dark casing (`--gmt-globe-casing`) at full strength, in both renderers (`globe/casing.ts`; `labelRingShift`). Inks (`--gmt-globe-ink`, `-marker`, `-selected`, `-gold`) have no light value. `globe-contrast.test.ts` sweeps backdrops: labels 7:1, markers and outlines 3:1, and fails if any overlay token gains a light value.
- The smoke runs parity with the image withheld, so it compares vector layers and doubles as the failed-load check. The image must be requested once in dark and on the light hero, and never on other light globes, canvas-2D, raised contrast, forced colours or reference pages. At 5x the globe must match its flat rendering.
