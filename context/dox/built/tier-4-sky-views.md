# Tier 4: Look at, names and events

Part of [built/index.md](index.md). Sky drawing: [tier-4-sky.md](tier-4-sky.md); the bodies it points at: [tier-4-sky-bodies.md](tier-4-sky-bodies.md); the zoom stack it sits in: [tier-4-globe.md](tier-4-globe.md).

## Look at (landing only)

- Look at (landing only): a kit `disclosure`, icon only, in the zoom stack's `role="group"` just before the info button (`lib/sky-view.ts`). It is secondary: its icon is `--gmt-ice-dim` at rest and a 4 px divider (3 px margin over the 1 px gap; inline on the phone row) sets it apart from the globe's own controls. Its panel is a second bar drawn like the stack (`widgets/tools/sky-look.module.css`): one button per row of `SKY_BODIES`, in the registry's order (the Sun, the Moon, then the planets outward from the Sun), each with its icon or astronomical symbol and its name, `aria-pressed` on the held body. Reason: the stack is where the camera controls already are, and a labelled bar reads at a glance where an icon-only second stack would not. The toggle is one of the stack's own buttons (`globe.module.css` styles every `button` under `.zoom`; `sky-look.module.css` undoes only what the disclosure adds, and its row rules weigh (0,3,0) so they beat the stack's square buttons and its hover fill). The stack and the bar lay the see-through border tint over the button surface, so a body behind them does not shine through the 1 px dividers. `.zoom` stops clipping while the bar is open so it can sit beside the stack, towards the globe, its foot level with the stack's (above the row on a coarse pointer). A planet is `aria-disabled`, its reason in `title`, while the planet layer loads or while it is too faint to be drawn; the rows never come and go, so the bar never jumps. The globe's tooltip is a top-layer popover that no z-index beats, so it hides while the bar is open. Choosing sets the lens zoom, then flies the one camera with the hop (the hop returns to the controller's goal zoom, so the zoom goes first), closes the bar and returns focus to the toggle, which stays cyan (`data-held`) while a body is held. The stack shows only with the sheet closed on a stacked phone, so the bar opens over the sky bay and the sheet is never touched. Hidden in forced colours with the sky.
- The aim (`lib/sky-aim.ts`, pure): the largest of zoom 2 to 1 whose spot beside the limb is 16 px inside the Earth's area and clear of the copy, open drawer and zoom stack, found by Newton's method on the rotation; the Moon is aimed through `moonFromCamera`, since its parallax is about a degree. No spot: look past the Earth at the body at zoom 1. `heroAimFrame` (`lib/hero-space.ts`) measures in the hero's `[data-space]`, not the hero: stacked, the hero runs the page's full height and the Earth's row ends at the fold, so a body is never put under the pages. Stacked, the copy column is `display: contents` and has no box, so the aim and the meteor keep clear of each of its cells instead. The open bar is one more box to keep clear of (`globe-mount.ts` passes it to `heroAimFrame`).
- The hold: on globe frames, at most once a second of the sky's clock, never mid-flight; a body 8 px off its spot, or a box that meets something, re-aims without a hop. `engine.holdAmbient(true)` keeps the idle spin off while the view is on (15 lines in the controller); release schedules it `IDLE_MS` later. The spin only runs at rest zoom, so after a release above zoom 1 it returns once the reader zooms out.
- Release never moves the camera: choosing the held body again, a drag, pinch, wheel, tap or key, a zoom-stack button or a picked zone. Reason: the reader's own move wins over the view.
- Names (landing only, `lib/sky-names.ts`): a pointer over bare sky (the hero, `[data-space]` or `globe-hit`) within 12 px of a body's disc for a mouse, 28 px for a touch, shows its name in a glass chip above it, a point's with its dim group (" · Planet", " · Asteroid", the registry row's `group`); below it within 48 px of the window's top, held 4 px inside the window's sides. Reason: northguild.dev names its sky the same way. The chip is a manual popover (the zone card's pattern), so no hero transform or clip moves it, placed in window pixels and hidden on scroll. Only drawn bodies are named: a planet the layer leaves out, a body wholly inside the Earth's disc, a planet inside the Moon's. The nearest disc edge wins, and a tie goes to the later row, so the Moon wins over the Sun it covers. It follows on `engine.onFrame`; a drag, any globe gesture but a tap, the copy or a control, leaving the window (not a lifted finger: a tap's name stays until the next tap) and forced colours take it away. `aria-hidden`, since Look at names the same bodies to every reader.
- Events: a body is "in view" when V is at most the layer's faint limit (5.5) from the Earth's centre; a rare event is that for a body the sky does not normally draw (a small body, a close pass), never a planet. Reason: the layer draws to 5.5, so the list promises exactly what the reader will see; the popover lists rare ones only (planets are always up or not worth a row).
- `scripts/prepare-sky-orbits.ts --events` writes `src/assets/sky-events.json` (`{spanEndMs, events}`, 3,650 days from gmt's clock): a step a day for planets and small bodies, 10 minutes for passes, each edge refined to the hour or the minute, with the same magnitude law the layer uses (`viewMagnitude`). Reason: nothing is searched in the reader's browser, and the file loads only when the info or Look at button is reached.
- A body that is not a planet says what it is beside its name in the Look at bar and the events list ("Vesta Asteroid", "Vesta · Asteroid · 8 July – 2 August 2029"; `groupTag`); planets stay bare. Reason: the planets are the sky's ordinary points, and an asteroid's name alone does not say what it is.
- Wording lives in `lib/sky-events.ts` alone (rows, Look at's second line, the ring's reason; it takes the data and gmt's formatters from `sky-events-kit.ts`, so the Look at bar loads it with the page); times are the reader's zone, in their first English locale else `en-GB`. Reason: one place to change a sentence, and a test or gate can hold it.
- The Look at bar offers every body the layer can place (`PointSource.placed`), faint ones too; the held faint body gets one 8 px ring (`id ring x y V` in `data-sky-planets`) and a ring label from sky-names. Reason: a reader who picks Uranus sees it is too faint, not missing, and nothing else gets a circle.
