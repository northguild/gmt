# Tier 4: planets, small bodies and close passes

Part of [built/index.md](index.md). Sky drawing: [tier-4-sky.md](tier-4-sky.md); Look at and events: [tier-4-sky-views.md](tier-4-sky-views.md).

## Planets, small bodies and passes

- Small bodies (`lib/kepler.ts`, `sky-orbits.json` `small`): Vesta is placed from the Minor Planet Center's osculating elements by two-body motion, within 720 days of their epoch, and drawn by the planets' own path (`hgMagnitude` for its V; Horizons' fitted G is 0.32 against the file's default 0.15). A body is in the file only if its peak magnitude `H + 5 log10(q(q-1))` is at or under 5.5, its perihelion `q` is 1.3 au or more, and it is not a comet. Refresh each year: pin the new year's `Soft00Bright.txt` in `scripts/prepare-sky-orbits.ts`, then `pnpm exec tsx scripts/prepare-sky-orbits.ts --small --mpc Soft00Bright.txt` (the planets part stays byte-identical). The weekly gate `sky-orbits-fresh` fails within 90 days of the end of the elements' validity.
- Close passes (`passes` in `sky-orbits.json`, Apophis on 13 April 2029): `scripts/prepare-sky-orbits.ts` asks JPL's close-approach API for Earth approaches within 0.002 au for the next 3,650 days from gmt's clock and keeps a body only if, sampled at its Horizons rows from the camera's orbit with the sky's own geometry and magnitude law, it reaches 5.5. Its brightness window is every row at 6.0 or brighter plus 30 minutes, so no hand-kept list and no date lives in code. The cheap bound (H + 5 log10 of the range, phase 0) only prefilters: it keeps 2001 WN5 (2028), which the exact bound drops (its best is 6.47 from the camera's orbit). A body that qualifies with no fitted G stops the run: G is never guessed. The code is in `scripts/lib/` (`close-approach`, `passes`, `pass-sets`, `small-bodies`), so `prepare-sky-orbits.ts` stays under 400 lines.
- The source is JPL's Small-Body Database solution (today 220), labelled a national peer in `PASS_ORBITS`: the IAU Minor Planet Center publishes elements for Apophis but no ephemeris through a pass at six Earth radii, where the Earth bends the path by tens of degrees, and an n-body integrator would need a planetary ephemeris that is itself national. The table is Horizons' vectors, UT rows (its leap-second table is in the data: 69 s is about 500 km at 7.4 km/s), 20 minutes apart from a day before the brightness window to a day after, cubic Hermite between rows (`lib/pass-table.ts`). `sky-orbits.json` keeps each pass's table window, H and G, and its element sets; the rows are the lazy chunk `lib/sky-pass.ts` with `sky-passes.json`, which the planets layer imports on the first draw inside the table window (the body is drawn from the next draw), because the table must not ride in every visitor's planets chunk. Look at's row for a pass is available exactly when the layer lists the body, as for every point. API answers carry a timestamp, so they are not hash-pinned: `sky-orbits.source.json` records the queries and the solution, and the generator's tests run on recorded answers in `scripts/fixtures/sky-orbits/`.
- A pass is placed at every instant from the run instant to the span's end. `passes.<id>.sets` (`PassSet`: Kepler elements and `fromMs` to `toMs`) are Horizons' osculating elements of the same solution, used outside the table; the table is used inside it, and a body with no table and no set at an instant has no place. Two-body elements fit an integrated orbit only for a while, so `scripts/lib/pass-sets.ts` picks the sets by measurement: candidate epochs (midnights every 60 days, and a ladder of 1 to 32 days either side of the table) are fetched with Horizons' ELEMENTS table (the reply must name the recorded solution), each is measured against Horizons' light-time-corrected geocentric vectors from the camera's orbit point nearest the body with the sky's own `bodyFromCamera` and precession, and from each stretch's start (the run instant to the table, the table to the span's end) the candidate that stays within `SET_TOLERANCE` (0.035 degrees, under a pixel of the widest lens) longest is kept, handing over at the last sample it holds. The handoff instants and each set's worst error are in the sidecar's `passSource`; nothing is typed. A look at a pass far from its day reads "Too faint · 20.7 · Close pass 13 Apr 2029" (`lookLine`), and its ring's reason says a close pass makes it bright enough on that day.
- Positions are the camera's, never the geocentre's (`bodyFromCamera`): the table is geocentric and added to the Earth's place at the instant the light left, because the Earth's own 30 km/s over the light time is 9 km at 90,000 km and the pins hold within 2 km. Parallax between the geocentre and the camera reaches tens of degrees near closest approach; the magnitude is `hgMagnitude` from the camera. Outside the table the same light-time step and precession place the body from its set, and the camera is subtracted from it.
- `earthCover(camera, body)` answers behind, front or null from the camera's ray and the Earth's sphere, so nothing tests the screen disc. A body nearer than the Moon draws on a second canvas, `[data-sky-canvas="near"]`, above the globe (`.gmt-sky-near`, below the copy), so Apophis passes in front of the Earth, Moon and Sun; the planet layer clears and sizes it each draw, so `sky.ts` gains no bytes. `data-sky-planets` lists `id x y V`, `id behind V` (behind the image plane) or `id earth V` (hidden by the Earth).
- Freshness: `sky-orbits-fresh` (weekly tier only) also fails when SBDB's current solution id for a pass's body differs from the recorded one (a new solution moves the pass) and when a shipped window has ended. Refresh: run `pnpm exec tsx scripts/prepare-sky-orbits.ts --small` from `apps/dox`, then the pins in `src/test/apophis-pins.ts` against Horizons again.
- Pluto (`small.pluto`, group `Dwarf planet`, symbol U+2647, a registry row after Neptune): its elements are its row of the IAU Minor Planet Center's distant minor planets file (`readDistant` in `scripts/lib/small-bodies.ts`, an international source; the file's bytes and sha256 are in the sidecar's `distantSource`), two-body for `ELEMENT_DAYS` either side of the epoch like Vesta's. The H, G system does not fit Pluto (0.42 to 0.57 magnitude too faint against Horizons), and no IAU law exists, so its magnitude is the Astronomical Almanac's `V = -1.01 + 5 log10(r delta)` (Urban and Seidelmann, Explanatory Supplement to the Astronomical Almanac, 3rd ed., table 10.6; `DWARF_ABSOLUTE` in `magnitude.ts`), pinned within 0.07 mag of Horizons' APmag (14.606 on 2026-11-27). At about 14.6 it is never drawn and never has a spell, but the Look at bar places it and a held view rings it. Its name tip reads "Pluto · Dwarf planet · The ninth planet from 1930 to 2006" (`rowNote` in `sky-events.ts`, third line of `sky-names.ts`'s label). `DISTANT_ORBITS` credits the file and the law.
- `providePoints(source)` (`lib/sky-registry.ts`) is how the lazy planet layer hands itself over once loaded (and takes itself back with `null` when its sky goes); `pointSource()` reads it, so the Look at bar, the names and the ring ask `place`, `placed` and `drawn` without importing the layer. Before it loads there is no source and every row reads as loading.
- The planets' series are VSOP87B (Bretagnon and Francou, IMCCE) cut to the generator's run instant plus and minus 200 years: `span` in `sky-orbits.json` is that range in epoch milliseconds, and the sky places no planet outside it. `scripts/prepare-sky-orbits.ts` records each planet's measured worst error against the full theory. It reads every planet but the Earth, Neptune included, however faint its peak: Look at offers a body the sky places while it is too faint to draw.
- Magnitudes (`lib/magnitude.ts`) are Mallama and Hilton 2018 as their reference code `Ap_Mag_V3.f90` computes them: Saturn's globe-alone law with its effective ring tilt, Uranus's planetographic sub-Earth and sub-Sun latitudes (WGCCRE 2015 radii) and Neptune's dated law. Mars adds the markings of Mallama 2007 (Tables 6 and 8, by Stirling interpolation): the central meridian is the mean of the sub-Earth and sub-Sun west longitudes from its WGCCRE rotation row at the instant the light left, and the season is the Sun's heliocentric J2000 ecliptic longitude minus 85 degrees. `planet-horizons.test.ts` holds every planet to its JPL Horizons pins within 0.002 magnitude, with position, distance and phase, and Mars's meridian to Horizons' ObsSub-LON. Horizons' Neptune stops at 2199, so it has no late pin. Reason: the reference code is the authors' own statement of the laws, so a reader can check each term.
- `lib/nutation.ts` holds Meeus ch. 22's four largest terms (the camera at the true equator of date); only the lazy pass chunk carries it, because the 3.5 km it moves a camera matters only for a pass 4,000 km off. A new use outside that chunk would put it in the planets' budget.
- `node scripts/sky-at.mjs <UTC instant> [<width>x<height>] [--base <url>]` opens a headed Chromium on the landing at an instant (gmt-parsed, `page.clock.install`) so a pinned moment can be looked at by hand; the default base is the dev server.
