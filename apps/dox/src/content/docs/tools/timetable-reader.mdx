A timetable prints wall times with no offset. The zone it is printed in is
what makes each one exact: `scheduleDelivery`'s `startTimeZone` reads a
printed departure as a published local time, resolves a repeated hour to the
earlier instant and a skipped hour to the later one, and lets a written
offset pick the other pass of a repeated hour.

This tool is about reading a transport timetable. For the general rule
behind a repeated or skipped local time in any zone, see the
[DST Inspector](/tools/dst-inspector/).

Reference: [`scheduleDelivery`](/reference/transport/calculate/scheduleDelivery/)
reads every row here, through its `startTimeZone` option, the same way
[`resolveLocal`](/reference/instant/convert/resolveLocal/) resolves a wall
time on its own. See the guide's
[section on reading a timetable](/guides/industries/transport-multi-leg-scheduling/#a-departure-must-be-exact-and-a-timetable-names-its-zone).
