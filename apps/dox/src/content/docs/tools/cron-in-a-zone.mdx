<Fragment slot="keypoint">

A cron line has no daylight-saving rule until someone states one. GMT makes you choose.

</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="intro">

A cron line says `30 1 * * *`, and a clock that skips 01:30 or shows it twice does not say what happens. [`cronRuns`](/reference/recurrence/calculate/cronRuns/) takes a policy for that night and has no default: `"crontabMatch"` or `"daemonCatchUp"`.

Type a line, pick a zone and a year, or press one of the Try choices. Section 1 shows the five fields the line parsed into. Section 2 draws the six hours around one clock change, one lane for each rule, with a mark at every run. Section 3 lists the next twelve runs under each rule and flags a run that only one rule gives. Section 4 prints every call made and what it returned. Every value on the page is a library result.

</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="after">

**Two documented rules.** POSIX (ISO/IEC/IEEE 9945) defines the five fields and says nothing about time zones. The crontab(5) page of cronie gives the matching rule: the schedule is compared with each wall time, so a skipped time never matches and a repeated time matches twice. The cron(8) page of cronie, and Debian's, gives the daemon rule: for a gap under 3 hours or an overlap of 3 hours or less, a job with a fixed time runs once, soon after the change. It applies only to a job with no `*` in the minute or hour field.

**Why `*/5` and `30 2` differ on the same night.** In Sydney the clocks jump from 02:00 to 03:00. `*/5 * * * *` has a `*` in its fields, so the daemon rule does not apply and both lanes are the same. `30 2 * * *` is a fixed time that falls inside the gap, so the two lanes differ.

**The day rule.** When both day fields are restricted, a day that matches either one runs: `0 0 1,15 * 1` runs on the 1st, the 15th and every Monday. A `*` or `*/n` counts as not restricted, and the month always has to match. POSIX words the rule so that a restricted month also counts as a reason to take either; no shipped cron does that, and neither does GMT. See [which days a cron line runs on](/guides/zoned-date-operations/recurrence-and-cron/#which-days-a-cron-line-runs-on).

**Weekday numbers.** `dayOfWeek` is stored as ISO weekday numbers, 1 for Monday to 7 for Sunday. Cron's `0` and `7` both read as `7`, so `0 0 * * 0` shows `7`.

**The choice is still yours.** Kubernetes and GitHub Actions take a zone for a schedule, and neither tells you which rule applies on the night the clocks change. Pick the policy your scheduler follows and say so in the call. The guide is [two rules for the night the clocks change](/guides/zoned-date-operations/recurrence-and-cron/#two-rules-for-the-night-the-clocks-change).
