"Two days before" reads three ways, and they only agree by accident.
Calendar days keep the departure's time of day; exact hours are elapsed
time, whatever the clock does; a pinned local time keeps the wall clock you
name and lets the elapsed time move. Between them, a DST change can make the
three land an hour apart, or make two of them coincide.

<CutoffRuler />

**Worth trying:** [no clock change in
between](/tools/cutoff-ruler/?w=cutoffruler&wa=%7B%22anchor%22%3A%222024-06-14T16%3A00%3A00Z%22%2C%22timeZone%22%3A%22Europe%2FAmsterdam%22%2C%22days%22%3A%222%22%2C%22atLocalTime%22%3A%2217%3A00%22%7D) —
calendar days and exact hours land on the same 18:00, and only the pinned
reading differs. Then [the spring-forward, where two readings
coincide](/tools/cutoff-ruler/?w=cutoffruler&wa=%7B%22anchor%22%3A%222024-03-11T22%3A00%3A00Z%22%2C%22timeZone%22%3A%22America%2FNew_York%22%2C%22days%22%3A%222%22%2C%22atLocalTime%22%3A%2217%3A00%22%7D) —
48 exact hours and the pinned 17:00 agree, by accident, and calendar days is
the odd one out at 47 hours.

Reference: [`cutoffAt`](/reference/transport/calculate/cutoffAt/) is the one
call behind every reading here. See the guide's
[two-days-before section](/guides/industries/transport-legs-and-dwell/#two-days-before-is-not-48-hours-cutoffat),
the scenario
[Two Days Before Is Not 48 Hours](/scenarios/two-days-before-is-not-48-hours/),
and the [DST Inspector](/tools/dst-inspector/) for the general rule behind
every gap and overlap a clock change makes.
