Twice a year most timezones skip an hour or repeat one. Inside the skipped hour a
local time simply does not exist; inside the repeated one it happens twice. Both
cases are real, both are common, and a date library has to answer for them
somehow.

Pick a zone and a year to see its transitions, then drag the ticker into a gap or
an overlap and watch
[`convertPlainDateTimeToZoned`](/reference/zoned/convert/convertPlainDateTimeToZoned/)
resolve the same local wall-clock time four different ways, one per
`disambiguation` option.

Boundary functions like `startOfZoned` are a different case: they always return
the real boundary — the earliest instant of the unit — and take no
disambiguation option at all. Disambiguation only applies to *field-setting*
functions, which is what resolving a specific probed wall time is.

<DstInspector />

**See it in action:** [1:30am on 3 November 2024 in New
York](/tools/dst-inspector/?w=dst&wa=%7B%22zone%22%3A%22America%2FNew_York%22%2C%22year%22%3A2024%2C%22preset%22%3A%22overlap%22%7D)
happens twice — scrub across the transition and switch `disambiguation` between
`earlier`, `later` and `reject` to see which instant each one picks.

Reference: [`getDstTransitions`](/reference/zoned/get/getDstTransitions/) finds
the transitions; [`convertPlainDateTimeToZoned`](/reference/zoned/convert/convertPlainDateTimeToZoned/)
resolves the probe.
