The core of GMT is a set of general primitives: zoned arithmetic, real day boundaries,
half-open intervals, business calendars. Each industry layer is a small set of functions
that name the operation an industry actually performs, built on those primitives so every
layer agrees about what a midnight, a day or an elapsed hour is.

Three things hold across every industry layer:

- **Facts come from the caller.** A port, airport, terminal or station code is not resolved
  to a time zone, and a contract term is not looked up by carrier. You pass the IANA zone and
  the terms; GMT does the time arithmetic. That keeps the library free of registries that go
  stale.
- **Elapsed time and calendar days are different answers.** A leg is elapsed hours across
  whatever DST transition it crosses. A charge is counted in local calendar days at the place
  the thing sat. The industry functions return both where both matter, and never derive one
  from the other.
- **Every result is a standard string.** The same ISO 8601 and RFC 9557 strings as the rest of
  the library, so an industry result feeds straight back into `interval/`, `zoned/` or a
  business calendar.

## Layers

- [Transport: legs and dwell](./transport-legs-and-dwell/) — add a leg's transit time to a
  departure, show an arrival where it lands, and measure dwell in exact hours and local
  calendar days. Shared by road, rail, sea and air.
- [Intermodal: free time and demurrage](./intermodal-free-time-and-demurrage/) — which clock a
  charge runs on, which day is free day one, calendar or working days, and the chargeable days
  and dates an invoice has to print. Built on the transport layer's day count.
